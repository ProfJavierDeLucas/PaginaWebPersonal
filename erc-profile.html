Geometric structures for differential equations and field theory

I develop geometric frameworks for PDEs and field theories, with emphasis on symmetry, reduction, integrability and non-conservative dynamics. A central direction is the use of k-contact geometry and jet geometry as a unifying language for field theories, transformations and geometric methods beyond the standard jet setting.

Research programme

The central question is whether modern contact-type and jet-type geometries can become a structural language for differential equations, field theories, transformations, reductions and integrability phenomena.

Research vision

My recent work develops a coherent programme around k-contact geometry, jet bundles, integrable systems, Hamilton–Jacobi theory and geometric reduction. The aim is to identify intrinsic geometric structures that simultaneously encode PDEs, non-conservative dynamics, symmetries, transformations and reduction procedures, rather than treating these topics through separate formalisms.

The programme is now moving from foundational structures to recognition results, transformations, reductions and applications to field theories and integrable systems.

Main research directions

1. k-contact geometry and field theories

Development of k-contact geometry as a framework for non-conservative field theories, reduction, Hamilton–Jacobi theory and dissipative PDE models.

2. Jets and PDE geometry

Recognition of jet bundles through higher-order polarised k-contact structures, with applications to symmetries, invariant submanifolds and transformations.

3. Integrable systems and transformations

Geometric study of integrability mechanisms, transformations, superposition phenomena, Lax-type structures and symmetry-based methods for differential equations.

4. Reduction and geometric mechanics

Marsden–Weinstein-type reductions, energy-momentum methods, stability, multisymplectic and related geometric structures.

Research group and training

Gamma Research Group

The Gamma Research Group is an internationally oriented research environment at the University of Warsaw, focused on geometric methods in mathematics and mathematical physics. It supports research on jets, symmetries, PDEs, field theories, geometric mechanics, integrable systems and modern reduction methods.

  • Weekly online Gamma seminar, with recorded talks and lecture series on the Gamma Seminar YouTube channel.
  • Hybrid Reading Groupoid sessions on Lie groupoids and Lie algebroids.
  • Monographic course on Geometric Mechanics II - Field Theory and student research projects.
  • Collaborations with the Universitat Rovira i Virgili in Tarragona, UPC Barcelona, CRM Montréal and other institutions.
Students and supervised theses

I have supervised more than 30 bachelor's and master's theses and student research projects, in addition to doctoral supervision. Several of my students have received research prizes and academic distinctions, and some former students now hold permanent faculty positions. Recent student topics include k-contact geometry, supersymplectic geometry, Cartan bundles, orbifold reduction, Lie group symmetries, vector bundle invariants, Clifford algebras, Lie algebroids, energy-momentum methods and energy-Casimir methods.
